What you’ll study

Power systems. Power electronics. Advanced electrification

Every student takes five required concentration courses, then chooses two concentration electives in areas such as power systems protection, power electronics and the Internet of Things.

  1. Power Systems Analysis and Design

    Model power grid components, including generators, motors, transformers, and transmission lines. Explore power flow analysis and economic dispatch methods, conduct short-circuit studies, and design protection schemes for electrical power systems.

  2. Power Electronics and Renewable Energy

    Explore distributed generation, renewable energy and energy storage technologies, and application of power electronics to electric utilities.

  3. Control Systems

    Design dynamic feedback loops and state controllers that keep power systems and electric machines stable and responsive under changing conditions.

Course highlights

Follow the Power

  • EGRE 371

    Introduction to Power Systems

    Explore how electricity is generated, transmitted, and delivered. Study power generation, electrical loads, transformers, rotating machines, transmission lines, and power networks while learning how engineers model and analyze the electrical grid.

  • EGRE 471

    Power System Analysis

    Explore how interconnected power systems operate and perform. Study power flow analysis, economic dispatch and fault analysis.

  • EGRE 573

    Sustainable and Efficient Power Systems

    Explore the engineering technologies shaping the future of energy. Study wind and solar power, energy storage, distributed generation, renewable energy integration, and energy efficiency. Evaluate the technical and economic feasibility of a renewable energy system.

  • EGRE 591

    Power Electronics

    Explore semiconductor devices, AC/DC, DC/DC, and DC/AC converter topologies, switching schemes, renewable energy resources, and energy storage systems. Model and analyze phase-locked loops (PLLs), closed-loop control, grid-connected converters, and variable frequency drives (VFDs). Utilize electromagnetic transient (EMT) software and Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) testing to evaluate system performance using metrics such as THD, TDD, Ripple Factor (RF), Form Factor (FF), and average values.

  • EGRE 592

    Power Systems Protection and Relaying

    Explore protection principles, instrument transformers, digital filtering, grounding schemes, and fault detection strategies. Analyze overcurrent, directional, differential, arc flash, and equipment-specific protection for buses, transformers, reactors, and capacitor banks within IEC 61850 framework standards. Apply ETAP software to perform short-circuit calculations and optimize protective device coordination.

FAQ

Questions students and parents ask

What can I do with a degree in electrical engineering with a power and energy systems concentration?

Graduates are prepared for roles such as power systems engineer, substation engineer, renewable energy engineer and grid integration engineer. Electric power generation, transmission and distribution is one of the largest industries for electrical engineers, and the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects 8% job growth for electrical and electronics engineers from 2025 to 2035. Dominion Energy was among the top employers of VCU’s May 2025 electrical engineering graduates.

Is there demand for power engineers?

Yes. “Demand for electric power is growing very rapidly,” said Robert Klenke, Ph.D., chair of VCU’s Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, and the power engineering workforce is aging. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects 8% job growth for electrical and electronics engineers from 2025 to 2035, much faster than average.

What classes will I take in VCU’s power and energy systems concentration?

Students in VCU’s B.S. in Electrical Engineering with a concentration in power and energy systems take five required concentration courses: Introduction to Electromagnetic Fields (EGRE 309), Introduction to Feedback Control Systems (EGRE 354), Introduction to Power Systems (EGRE 371), Power System Analysis (EGRE 471) and Sustainable and Efficient Power Systems (EGRE 573). They also choose two concentration electives in areas such as control systems design, embedded programming and the Internet of Things. The degree requires 127 credit hours.

Will I get hands-on experience in VCU’s power and energy systems concentration?

Yes. Every student completes a two-semester senior capstone (Senior Design Studio I and II) with at least six lab hours a week, and can complete it on a Vertically Integrated Projects (VIP) research team instead. Through VCU’s partnership with Dominion Energy, the program also includes internships, senior design projects and site visits.

Can I earn an accelerated master’s degree in the power and energy systems concentration at VCU?

Yes. Qualified B.S. in Electrical Engineering students can enter VCU’s accelerated pathway to the M.S. in Engineering with a concentration in electrical and computer engineering, taking graduate courses during their final undergraduate semesters. Up to 12 graduate credits earned as an undergraduate can count toward the master’s degree. Students work with their adviser to choose courses and get departmental pre-approval from the faculty program director.

Is VCU’s electrical engineering degree with a power and energy systems concentration ABET-accredited?

Yes. VCU’s B.S. in Electrical Engineering, including the power and energy systems concentration, is accredited by the Engineering Accreditation Commission of ABET under the General Criteria and Program Criteria for Electrical, Computer, Communications, Telecommunication(s), and Similarly Named Engineering Programs.
