What you’ll study

Four disciplines. Every kind of robot.

A robot is a body, a nervous system and a brain. You’ll learn to engineer all three and make them work together.

  1. Mechatronics and Control

    Wire up sensors, motors and controllers and make them work together as one system.

  2. AI and Autonomy

    Teach machines to sense their surroundings, make decisions and navigate on their own.

  3. Robot Programming and Embedded Systems

    Program microcontrollers and build robot software in Python and the Robot Operating System (ROS).

  4. Medical and Rehabilitation Robotics

    Apply robotics to prosthetics, exoskeletons and rehabilitation, drawing on VCU’s ties to its medical school.

Hands-on experience

Don’t just study robots. Build them.

  1. Introduction to Robotics and Autonomous Systems (ENGR 261), a first-year course with hands-on robot demonstrations and lab exercises
  2. Work with humanoid, quadruped, mobile and drone robots in the college’s new shared robotics space
  3. Undergraduate research through VCU’s Vertically Integrated Projects (VIP) program and labs like the Autonomous Robots and Vehicle Lab, home to an autonomous car, micro-drones, mobile robots, a humanoid robot and a driving simulator
  4. Project-based specialty courses in mechatronics, robotics, feedback control, AI and embedded systems
  5. Two-semester senior capstone where you design and build a working robotic or autonomous system, advised by project sponsors

Course highlights

From first robot to final build

  • ENGR 261

    Introduction to Robotics and Autonomous Systems

    Get a hands-on introduction to the pieces that make robots work. Demonstrations and lab exercises cover robotic components, programming and real-world applications.

  • ENGR 262

    Foundations of Robotic Programming

    Move from understanding robots to actually programming them. You’ll configure microcontrollers, connect sensors and actuators and build robotic applications using Python and the Robot Operating System.

  • ENGR 404 / 405

    Senior Design Studio I and II

    Spend your senior year turning a big idea into a working robotic or autonomous system. Your team designs, builds, tests and validates the project over two semesters, with at least six lab hours a week and advising from project sponsors.

FAQ

Questions students and parents ask

Is VCU the only university in Virginia with a robotics engineering degree?

Yes. VCU’s B.S. in Robotics and Autonomous Systems Engineering, approved by the State Council of Higher Education for Virginia (SCHEV) and launched in fall 2026, is the only bachelor’s degree of its kind in Virginia. It is offered by the VCU College of Engineering in Richmond and combines mechanical, electrical and computer engineering, computer science and artificial intelligence in one degree.

What jobs can I get with a robotics engineering degree from VCU?

VCU’s B.S. in Robotics and Autonomous Systems Engineering prepares students to design, integrate and deploy robotic and autonomous technologies across industries such as manufacturing, health care, defense and transportation. The curriculum combines robotics, AI, control systems, programming and engineering design to prepare students for work involving technologies such as industrial robots, autonomous vehicles and robotic medical systems. Graduates can pursue roles such as robotics software engineer, controls engineer and perception engineer, or continue to graduate school.

Is robotics engineering a good career as AI changes engineering jobs?

Robotics is where artificial intelligence meets the physical world, and industry leaders describe an “automation gap,” a shortage of engineers who understand both robot hardware and the software behind it. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics counts robotic engineers among mechanical engineers and projects 11% employment growth for that field from 2025 to 2035, much faster than average. VCU’s Robotics and Autonomous Systems Engineering program prepares students for roles such as robotics software engineer and perception engineer.

Is VCU’s Robotics and Autonomous Systems Engineering B.S. ABET-accredited?

Not yet. Like every new engineering program, VCU’s B.S. in Robotics and Autonomous Systems Engineering cannot be reviewed by ABET until it has graduates. The program launched in fall 2026, and VCU expects its first class to graduate in spring 2030. The College of Engineering plans to seek ABET accreditation after that first graduating class, with accreditation targeted for around 2031.

What classes will I take in VCU’s Robotics and Autonomous Systems Engineering degree?

Students in VCU’s program take courses in Robotics (EGMN 427), Mechatronics (EGMN 416), Artificial Intelligence (CMSC 436), Foundations of Robotic Programming (ENGR 262), Introduction to Feedback Control Systems (EGRE 354) and Introduction to Intelligent Autonomous Systems (EGRE 412). The curriculum also combines computer science, electrical engineering and mechanical engineering coursework, along with math, physics and engineering electives.

Will I get hands-on experience in VCU’s Robotics and Autonomous Systems program?

Yes. Robotics-specific courses in VCU’s Robotics and Autonomous Systems Engineering program include project-based and laboratory experiences, and students complete a two-semester senior capstone where they design, build, test and validate a working robotic or autonomous system.

How is robotics engineering different from mechanical, electrical or computer engineering?

Mechanical, electrical and computer engineering each focus on one part of a robot: the mechanical body, the electronics or the software. VCU’s B.S. in Robotics and Autonomous Systems Engineering combines all three with artificial intelligence, so students learn to design and integrate a complete system. Students who want to specialize in one area can also consider VCU’s mechanical, electrical or computer engineering degrees.
