Why it's OK to be undecided

Most high schools don't teach engineering.

Biomedical, chemical, computer, electrical, mechanical, nuclear: from the outside, these can all sound alike. The best way to tell them apart is to see the problems each one solves, meet the faculty, and try the coursework. That's exactly what your first semester as an Undeclared Engineering student is for.

Choosing a major is a big decision. Making it with more information isn't falling behind; it's a smart way to start.

"Undecided" doesn't mean unprepared. It means you're curious about more than one way to make an impact.

How it works

From "not sure yet" to your major

Here's what the path looks like for an Undeclared Engineering student.

Apply as Undeclared Engineering

When you apply to VCU, select Undeclared Engineering as your intended program. Your application is reviewed for admission to the College of Engineering, just like any other engineering applicant.

Build your foundation

Your first semester focuses on the math, science and introductory engineering courses that every engineering major builds on. [Course names to confirm]

Explore with an advisor

Meet regularly with an advisor who works with undeclared students. Visit labs, attend department info sessions, join student organizations and talk with upper-level students.

Declare your major

After your first semester, choose your field. Each department has its own requirements to declare, and your advisor will make sure you're on track to meet them.
